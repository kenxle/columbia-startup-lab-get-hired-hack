Why we built it

The same item arrives written many ways

Buyer A
304 SS sheet 16ga 4x8
Buyer B
Stainless 304, 0.060 in, 48 x 96

Both mean a sheet. But 16 gauge is not 0.060 in on a stainless table, and a wrong read becomes a wrong quote, a lost order, or a return.

Today a person decodes every line by hand:

Material and grade304, 304L, 316L
ThicknessA gauge only implies it, and gauge tables differ by material
SizeInches, feet, either order
StandardASTM A240, ASME SA-240
PaperworkMTR, COA, 3.1 cert, DFARS

RudCo's quote form promises a reply within 24 hours. That promise is a requirement in our build.

Accuracy and uncertainty

Every field shows its source and its rule

The gauge trap

"16 ga" on stainless reads as 0.0625 in, with the table named. The challenge's own example treats it as 0.060 in.

No price without a named, dated benchmark

A line with no benchmark is held and left out of the total.

Compliance flags with trigger words

"SA-240 316L 3/8 plate, with 3.1 cert" raises four flags: ASME code material, 3.1 certificate, MTR and COA. Two are shown.

A flag records what the buyer needs. It does not certify RudCo's stock.

Accuracy and uncertainty

Ask instead of guess. The rep approves.

The buyer wrote a thickness that is not a gauge

Stainless 304, 0.060 in, 48 x 96, 10 sheets

The question that goes out

Line 3 says 0.060 in. 16 gauge stainless is 0.0625 in. Do you need exactly 0.060 in, or is 16 gauge fine?

Questions go out on their own and are tracked on the line. Clear lines keep moving.
