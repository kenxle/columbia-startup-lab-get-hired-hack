# RudCo: Company Profile

**Status:** Approved by Ken, 2026-10-10.

Researched 2026-10-10 from public pages only. Where a fact came from a search-result snippet and not from a page I read, it is marked **(snippet only)**. Text in quotation marks is exact wording I read on the linked page.

## What matters to them

- **Documents travel with the material.** RudCo's site sells "PO→COA/MTR" traceability (a purchase order tied to a certificate of analysis and a mill test report) and "DFARS/REACH/RoHS flags" ([FAQ text in the site code](https://www.rud-co.com/assets/index-B04wKX3n.js)). Their own pages say each shipment's certificates and country-of-origin records are tied back to the PO, lot and heat number. Our compliance-flag step (mill test reports, country of origin, spec certifications) is the thing they already promise customers.
- **Buyers can see a fair price.** Their LinkedIn page says "Opacity is a tax on industrial procurement." ([RudCo LinkedIn post, 2026-04-20](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/www%2Erud-co%2Ecom_opacity-is-a-tax-on-industrial-procurement-activity-7451955371537059840-lsR0)). Another post asks "Have you ever wondered if you're getting the right rate?" ([post, 2026-05-11](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/www%2Erud-co%2Ecom_have-you-ever-wondered-if-youre-getting-activity-7459610821522833408--F-1)). A price shown next to a named benchmark, with a reason, matches how they talk.
- **Quotation intake is a named pain.** In the founder's Digital Journal interview, procurement teams may spend time requesting quotations by email, and he believes automation can help "organize routine work such as quotation intake" (see [the article](https://www.digitaljournal.com/article/ai-infrastructure-boom-reshapes-industrial-materials-procurement/)). Their company page says "Procurement is still on email." ([post, 2026-08-25](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/www%2Erud-co%2Ecom_ai-infrastructure-boom-reshapes-industrial-activity-7497807860853059584-F2_C)). This track is their stated problem, not a side exercise.
- **AI supports the person; it does not replace them.** Founder quote: "People still want to deal with people, especially when the decision is important" and "Technology can handle more of the coordination and give those people better information for the conversation." ([Digital Journal, 2026-08-21](https://www.digitaljournal.com/article/ai-infrastructure-boom-reshapes-industrial-materials-procurement/)). This fits our rule that the rep approves every quote.
- **Speed and trust together.** The site's tagline is "Precision. Transparency. Trust." ([site code](https://www.rud-co.com/assets/index-B04wKX3n.js)). The FAQ promises "Stocked lines ship in 24–48 hours" (same file). The Columbia listing calls the offer "Fast, simple and easy material supply." ([Columbia directory](https://entrepreneurship.columbia.edu/?p=81629)).
- **They are very small.** LinkedIn's company data lists 3 employees ([RudCo LinkedIn page](https://www.linkedin.com/company/www.rud-co.com), structured data). The person judging you is probably the founder or someone close to him, and the right hire for a three-person company is someone who ships a working thing.

**What they sell.** The site lists abrasives (steel shot and grit, garnet, aluminum oxide, corn cob, glass bead, stainless abrasives) and metals (primary aluminum P1020, 356 alloys, titanium, steel billets and plate, copper cathode, master alloys, fluxes, ferro alloys) ([site code](https://www.rud-co.com/assets/index-B04wKX3n.js)). A recycling page covers scrap aluminum and spent abrasive media.

**How they make money.** They look like a software-enabled supplier: they source from producers and smelters and sell to manufacturers, with a web front end on top. Evidence:

- Their terms speak of "placing an order," "receiving a quote," invoices and past-due charges, so RudCo itself sells and invoices ([terms text in site code](https://www.rud-co.com/assets/index-B04wKX3n.js)).
- The metals page says primary aluminum comes "from multiple smelters" with COA/MTR on every shipment (same file), which is a reseller or program-supply position.
- The founder describes "a technology-supported procurement model designed to organize pricing, availability, and fulfillment information" and work that includes "providing data and information, sourcing and supplying" materials, plus logistics and recycling (paraphrase from [Digital Journal](https://www.digitaljournal.com/article/ai-infrastructure-boom-reshapes-industrial-materials-procurement/)).
- The site claims a portal with product search, "real-time availability," "dynamic pricing," and ordering "directly through the portal." The product schema on the pages shows a price of "0" and in-stock for every item (site code), which reads as placeholder data. I did not log in or test the portal, so treat the portal claims as marketing until seen.
- A quote request is a four-step form that promises a reply "within 24 hours" (site code). Business hours are listed as Monday to Friday, 7:00 AM to 8:00 PM EST. A real person still builds the quote today.

**Who buys.** The site lists energy and oil/gas, foundries, machine and fabrication shops, OEMs and heavy manufacturing, paint and coatings prep, pipe and tube, and steel mills ([site](https://www.rud-co.com/)). LinkedIn says customers "range from $2M to $10B in revenue across defense primes, aerospace, automotive, and construction" ([LinkedIn page](https://www.linkedin.com/company/www.rud-co.com)). That is RudCo's own claim. I found no named customer, and the site's one case study link ("How Steel Mill Reduced Costs 22% with Smart Procurement") points to a placeholder.

## What this means for our demo and pitch

How the RFQ track maps to their business: RFQs are how RudCo's buyers ask for price, and the founder names email quote intake as manual work. A faster, more accurate quote helps a three-person supplier handle more buyers without more staff.

- **Show the compliance flags early.** Documents and traceability are the most repeated selling point on their site. Make the mill test report, country of origin and spec certification flags easy to see on the first screen of the demo.
- **Show the benchmark next to every price.** "Opacity is a tax" is their phrase. A price with a named benchmark and a one-sentence reason answers it directly. Label the data as sample data on screen, as the brief commits to.
- **Keep the rep in charge.** Their founder says people stay central and technology gives them "better information for the conversation." Our "draft, rep approves, buyer questions go out" flow says the same thing. Use that framing in the pitch: the agent prepares the conversation, the rep has it.
- **Lean on "ask, don't guess."** It matches both the Devpost rule on uncertainty and the founder's point about specialized grades. A clarifying question that offers the buyer a choice (16 gauge or 0.060 in) shows you understand real buying.
- **Use their materials.** Their catalog is steel shot, abrasives, aluminum alloys, titanium, copper cathode and steel plate. The Devpost example is stainless sheet, which is outside their listed range. Mix at least a few RudCo-style lines (steel shot S-330, P1020 ingot, aluminum 356) into the test RFQs so the demo looks like their business. The catalog sizes and grades named on their site are in the [site code](https://www.rud-co.com/assets/index-B04wKX3n.js).
- **Mention speed in their terms.** They promise a reply within 24 hours and shipping in 24 to 48 hours for stocked items. A quote draft in minutes fits that story.
- **Keep it a working product.** Three people, builder-minded. Devpost rejects "thin wrappers that pass a prompt to a model and print the result." A demo that shows correct line parsing and an honest "no match" beats a wide feature list.
