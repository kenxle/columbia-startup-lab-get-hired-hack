# Voice of User: Other Sources

Track: voice of user from sources the open-web and Reddit tracks did not try. Run 2026-10-10. Covers software review sites, job postings, podcasts, Hacker News and founder posts, employee reviews, and surveys. No Reddit pages were fetched.

## Read this first

**The answer.** The best new evidence is job postings and one distributor case study. Software review sites gave little, and no podcast, Hacker News thread, or survey gave usable first-person quoting voice. What the new sources add:

- **Job postings show the steps and the checks.** Two postings (Precision Steel Services and Nox Metals) spell out what a quoting rep does: read the request, confirm dimensions and grade, catch special requirements (testing, inspection, tolerances, origin restrictions, cert requirements), document assumptions, and review every quote before it goes out. They are the closest thing found to a metals quoting rep describing the job. They are employer wording, not rep wording.
- **One named inside sales manager speaks in his own words.** Travis Bryant of Puget Sound Pipe & Supply (a pipe, valve and fitting distributor, not a metals service center) says in a vendor case study that a 100-line bid took an hour to an hour and a half, and that he has typed some items "tens of thousands of times" and still made mistakes. It is the only first-person rep voice in this track, and the vendor (Canals) published it.
- **Customers' shorthand and approved lists are real.** The same case study says requests arrive as Excel files full of shorthand and as photos of handwritten lists, and that oil and gas customers required approved product lists and preferred domestic items.
- **Review sites say little about quoting.** The one clear signal is Epicor Prophet 21 (a distribution ERP): TrustRadius's summary of 29 reviews lists an outdated, inefficient quoting process as a con.
- **No hit-rate or quote-turnaround survey exists in what I could read.** The NAW/MDM report is paywalled. The one buyer survey I found (Paperless Parts, 2022) is from a software vendor and I saw it only in a search summary.

**Gaps, stated up front.**

- G2 and Capterra block automated browsers. Capterra showed a bot-check page and G2 returned no text. I did not try to get past either. I read G2 reviews through SaaSworthy, which republishes them and labels the source as G2.
- No reviews exist on the pages I could reach for Enmark/ENITEO, Invera, Infor, Eclipse, Proton, Mercura, Hexa or Avent. Details in the coverage table.
- No podcast or video transcript was readable. Show notes only.
- No Hacker News thread had a founder relaying what metals or industrial distributors told them.
- Employee reviews on Indeed mention inside sales but nothing about quoting.

**How to read the evidence labels.**

- **Read directly** means I read the page text in a browser. Words in quotation marks from these pages are exact.
- **Via fetch tool** means a summarizing tool returned the page content. I paraphrase these and do not use quotation marks, except where noted.
- **Search summary only** means I saw the item only in a search result snippet. It is a lead, not a verified fact.
- **Vendor** means the publisher sells quoting or ERP software. **Secondhand** means a founder, vendor or writer is relaying what customers said.

## Question 0: The job steps, who does them, where time goes, what a better quote wins

### The steps, as two job postings describe them (read directly)

**Precision Steel Services** (Toledo, OH; a steel supplier since 1974, per its own posting; posting does not show a date): [Estimator - Steel Plate Quoting & Pricing](https://jobs.workable.com/jobs/0862cc67-073f-4e64-8eb0-0057c054e3bf.md). The "What You'll Do" list is, in order:

1. "Review customer requests, emails, prints, and specifications to fully understand what the customer is asking for"
2. "Verify that quote information is complete and accurate before pricing: dimensions, grades, quantities, and details"
3. "Identify special requirements such as testing, inspection, tolerances, or origin restrictions, and confirm them with the shop before quoting"
4. "Review customer history and win/loss patterns to set a competitive price position"
5. "Evaluate how parts fit standard stock sizes and account for unusable remnant and waste"
6. "Build and verify pricing in our pricing software, including material cost, markup, and processing costs such as burning"
7. "Recognize material we don't stock or processes we can't run in-house, and route those quotes correctly"
8. "Spot jobs that exceed volume, weight, or dollar thresholds and follow the defined escalation path"
9. "Document assumptions, lead times, and any changes clearly on the quote so the customer is never surprised"
10. "Run a final review of every quote for accuracy, minimums, and total-cost sanity before sending"

The same posting then says who hands off to whom: the estimator passes "value-add and time-critical information to Account Management so they can win the order." It also defines success: "Special requirements are verified and written on the quote before it is sent."

**Nox Metals** (Detroit; aluminum plate, bar and rounds for aerospace and defense; the posting is closed): [Sales Development Representative (SDR)](https://startup.jobs/sales-development-representative-sdr-nox-metals-8027208). Nox calls itself a technology company, so this is a software-forward shop, but the duties are the same job. The listed duties:

- "Own inbound RFQs and quoting end to end, every quote out the door fast and accurate"
- "Translate customer drawings, specs, and POs into clean, complete jobs in NOX NEST"
- "Push orders into production with every detail right, alloy, temper, thickness, tolerances, cert requirements, ship date, packaging"
- "Catch issues before they become problems, missing specs, mismatched POs, unrealistic dates, conflicting cert requirements"
- "Always ask questions, never guess when something is unclear"
- "Capture every quote, every win, every loss in our CRM and quoting system, the data is the moat"

### Who does each step

- At Precision Steel, the estimator does intake through the final check. Account Management makes the follow-up call, and "the shop" confirms special requirements and capability. Source: the posting above.
- At Nox, one inside rep owns the RFQ from intake through production handoff, then keeps the customer relationship "inside the four walls." Source: Nox posting above.
- Other postings (via fetch tool, thinner): A.M. Castle's inside sales role includes quoting prices and committing to delivery dates and checking material availability and conformance to customer requirements ([jobvite](https://jobs.jobvite.com/castlemetals/job/oCyDAfw7)). Boone Steel's inside sales role takes requests by phone, email and counter and develops pricing ([betterteam](https://boonesteel.betterteam.com/inside-sales-person-metal-service-center-1)). Vulcan, an Australian metals distributor, has an estimator and internal sales role that quotes from CAD/PDF drawings and supplies test certs ([Workable](https://jobs.workable.com/jobs/fafd3b62-c40c-4638-81c9-eea42cdbbe1b.md)). Valley Iron's inside sales role asks reps to review all quotes and orders for accuracy before sending ([InterviewPal](https://www.interviewpal.com/jobs/2354758609)).
- A search summary reports that an Aero Metals Alliance (Sunshine Metals) inside sales posting in Wichita sets a goal of answering each RFQ within 24 hours. I could not open the posting (KansasWorks returned an error), so treat this as a lead: [KansasWorks search result](https://www.kansasworks.com/jobs/13460060).

Quotes were prepared "by hand, drawing from several systems," and "Time-to-Quote was typically hours and often required multiple clarifying communications between the customer and Ryerson's sales team." Vendor writing about a customer, no Ryerson person quoted, 2023: [Concurrency](https://partner.concurrency.com/) (read directly).
- **An industrial seals distributor.** Concurrency's other case study says manual quoting took over 3 hours per quote, with about 20 quote requests a day arriving through several channels. Vendor writing, no customer quoted, read via fetch tool: [Concurrency](https://concurrency.com/case-study/ai-auto-quoting-saves-man-hours-reduces-missed-opportunities/).

## What this evidence says about the four angles

**1. The quote shows its work (every field links back to the RFQ words). Strongest support in this track.**

- Both detailed postings make the check and the paper trail the center of the job: "Document assumptions, lead times, and any changes clearly on the quote so the customer is never surprised," "Run a final review of every quote," "every assumption documented" (Nox), and "You never guess on pricing or capability. You verify it."
- A Paperless Parts reviewer praises the tool for catching what the human might overlook, and another says its estimated cycle time is "a little off at times." Trust depends on whether the user can see why a number is what it is.
- The Canals case study says the rep's remaining job is "reviewing and confirming item matches." Showing the source words next to each match fits that step.
- Gap: no one in these sources says "I wish the tool showed me where it got this." The support is inferred from the review step employers require.

**2. One-click buyer questions. Moderate support.**

- Ryerson's quotes "often required multiple clarifying communications between the customer and Ryerson's sales team" (vendor writing). Nox's "Always ask questions, never guess when something is unclear" and Precision Steel's "confirm them with the shop before quoting" both describe asking as a normal step.
- No source says buyers like or dislike being asked. The open-web track found a fabricator (Staub) saying stopping to ask slows quoting. Nothing here adds buyer-side evidence.
- Existing tools: nothing found that does this by name.

**3. Learns the buyer's shorthand. Real need, but a tool already claims it.**

- Puget Sound's case study describes Excel files of shorthand and handwritten photos, and says the AI "learns how each rep quotes and what specific customers need" (vendor claim). The user quote about "teaching" it comes from a named manager. Avent's founder says knowledge lives "in inboxes or people's heads."
- Risk for uniqueness: Canals (a funded vendor with a published customer story) already sells this for distributors. A judge who knows Canals will not see it as new. A demo that shows the learned reading carrying over to a second RFQ would still be a clear trust moment.
- Also note: the case study says early users "didn't get item matches right away" before it improved. Nothing in these sources measures how much it learns.

**4. Catches hidden requirements. Employers name it, no tool review mentions it.**

- Precision Steel puts "testing, inspection, tolerances, or origin restrictions" as a rep duty. Nox lists cert requirements, DFARS, AS9100, AMS and ASTM. Lapham-Hickey requires reading MTRs. Puget's oil and gas customers required approved product lists and domestic preference.
- No review, case study or founder post I read says an existing tool flags requirements the buyer did not state. Of the four angles, this is the one with the least vendor coverage in this track. That is weak evidence of a gap, because I only read a handful of vendor pages.
- Gap: no source gives how often or at what cost a cert is missed.

**A possible fifth angle, from the vendors' own claims only.** CloudForge says service centers follow up on only 10 to 15 percent of quotes (vendor claim, no source) and Puget says follow-up "always came last." Follow-up after the quote is sent is outside the brief's RFQ-to-sent-quote scope, and the only evidence is vendor-published, so I record it as background.

**What I would not rely on from this track.** All speed-wins-orders numbers come from vendors or from unnamed executives relayed by vendors. The only first-person rep voice is a vendor-published case study about a pipe distributor. The review-site evidence is thin and mostly from job shops or generic distribution ERP users.
